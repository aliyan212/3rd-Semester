## I. Introduction to Systems
**1. Analog vs. Digital Systems**
*   **Analog System:** Deals with analog quantities.
	*   _Characteristics:_ Continuous nature.
	*   _Examples:_ Temperature, Pressure, Flow, Speed, Mass.
*   **Digital System:** Deals with physical quantities in a modern context.
	*   _Characteristics:_ Discrete steps (e.g., a valve changing in specific steps rather than continuously).
**2. Why do we study Analog and Digital Systems?**
*   To understand the transition from continuous natural phenomena to discrete computing processing.
---
## II. Core Terminology
*   **Logic:** A set of principles or rules used for reasoning.
*   **Design:** A plan or strategy for the creation of products or services.
*   **Digital Circuit:** A specific type of design that implements logic rules using electronic components.
---
## III. Course Objective: What do we do in DLD?
**Primary Goal:** Design digital circuits using **Boolean Logic**.
**How to design digital systems:**
1.  **Machine Language:** Electronic machinery fundamentally understands only `0` and `1` (Binary).
2.  **Representation:** While machines use binary (`0`, `1`), humans often use **Hexadecimal** to make large binary numbers easier to understand and read.
3.  **Foundational Knowledge:** To master DLD, one must understand:
	*   Binary Systems
	*   Octal Systems
	*   Number Systems in general
---
## IV. Circuit Classifications
Digital logic is divided into two primary types of circuits:
1.  **Combinational Circuits:** Output depends only on the current input.
2.  **Sequential Circuits:** Output depends on current input and past history (memory).
